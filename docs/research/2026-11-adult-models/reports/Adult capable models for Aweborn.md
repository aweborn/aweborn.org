# Open-weight models open Aweborn's 18+ door

*Research report, 2026-11-07. Builds on the decisions in [phases/07-genai-creation.md § "18+ worlds, the Reddit way"](../../../../phases/07-genai-creation.md). Not legal advice.*

**Status markers used throughout:** **[Unverified]** means the claim rests on a secondary or marketing source, a search snippet, or background knowledge that wasn't re-fetched. **[Lawyer]** means counsel must answer it before 18+ ships. **[Confirm in writing]** means Aweborn should get a written statement from the provider. Prices and policies were pulled on 2026-11-07 and change often. Re-pull them before relying on any number.

Aweborn can offer a real "any model" menu for 18+ worlds. That menu will come mostly from **open-weight models on hosts that permit adult content and offer zero data retention**, not from the big labs. On OpenRouter with account-level ZDR, DeepSeek V4.1, GLM-5.3, Qwen 3.5, Kimi K3.1, Mistral, Llama 3.3 and a dozen roleplay finetunes cost about **$0.03–$2 per million input tokens**. Anthropic and OpenAI forbid erotica, and OpenRouter runs its own filter in front of their endpoints. That matters because Aweborn's own planner runs on Claude. Explicit stylized images have to leave OpenRouter's mostly closed image catalog for an open-weight host (fal, Replicate, Runware) or a rented GPU. Explicit 3D is effectively self-host-only, because Meshy and Rodin ban pornography. ElevenLabs' current policy reads as allowing consensual adult erotic *speech*, but Suno and Stability ban explicit audio. On safety, hash matching is free or nearly free but **almost useless against freshly generated content**, and no vendor documents an apparent-age classifier for anime or cartoon art. So the "minors in any style" hard line needs a custom gate: a guard model, a vision judge with a written rubric, and human review. For age checks, the research **widens the list of places where a real check is required** beyond the UK and the one-third US states. Australia (which names sexual AI companion chatbots), France, Italy, Brazil, probably Germany, and US states without a one-third threshold all need a real check or a geoblock. The biggest open items are data-retention terms for every image, 3D and voice host (none were found), the license status of the best anime models, and several legal questions only a lawyer can settle.

## Text: OpenRouter permits adult fiction, but only open-weight hosts will write it

OpenRouter's own Terms (updated Aug 31, 2026) **say nothing specific about sexual content**. They require users to be 18+, ban unlawful content and CSAM, and pass through each upstream provider's terms. The customer is "responsible for all acts and omissions of your Authorized Users, including any violation of applicable Model Terms", and OpenRouter can cut off a model at the provider's request ([OpenRouter Terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)). The binding limits are therefore legality, minors, and each model maker's acceptable-use policy (AUP). The live models API makes the split visible: `is_moderated` is **true for Anthropic Claude and most OpenAI GPT-5.x endpoints** and false for xAI, Google, Mistral, DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi, Qwen and the community finetunes ([OpenRouter models API](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models), pulled 2026-11-07).

The lab policies match that split. Anthropic's AUP, effective Nov 12, 2026, forbids depicting sex acts, fetish content and "erotic chats", and bans sexualizing minors "including in fictional settings or via roleplay" ([Anthropic AUP](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)). OpenAI delayed ChatGPT's adult mode again in March 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/07/openai-delays-chatgpts-adult-mode-again/)). Weak blogs say there was still no launch as of October ([justainews](https://justainews.com/companies/openai/adult-mode-in-chatgpt-explained-nsfw-erotica-porn-policy/)), and a new OpenAI usage policy dated Nov 20, 2026 is pending and unread **[Unverified]**. Mistral's Usage Policy (Jun 11, 2026) bans CSAM, non-consensual intimate images and sexual services, and says nothing about adult fiction in general ([Mistral Usage Policy](https://legal.mistral.ai/terms/usage-policy)). Meta's Llama 4 AUP bans child exploitation and distributing obscene material *to minors*, including failure to use legally required age-gating ([Llama 4 Use Policy](https://dev.meta.ai/llama/llama4/use-policy/)). That clause makes Aweborn's 18+ door part of license compliance for every Llama-based finetune. xAI's AUP page returned a 403. A secondary explainer says the Aug 14, 2026 version targets real people ("depicting real people pornographically", even with consent) and minors, not fiction in general ([Grokified](https://grokified.com/blog/grok-api-acceptable-use-policy)) **[Unverified]**.

There is a direct consequence for Aweborn's own code: **the Claude-based planner, minds and Haiku safety judge cannot produce sexual content for 18+ worlds**. In 18+ worlds, adult minds and adult lore have to route to a non-Anthropic model. Whether running explicit fiction through Haiku *only to classify it* fits Anthropic's AUP is not addressed in the sources **[Confirm in writing]** with Anthropic. Until that is confirmed, plan for an open-weight judge as a fallback.

Zero data retention (ZDR) is easy to enforce. The account-level setting routes only to endpoints with a no-retention policy, and if OpenRouter can't confirm an endpoint's policy, it assumes the endpoint keeps data. **1,120 endpoints across about 66 providers qualify** ([OpenRouter ZDR docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/zdr); [ZDR endpoints API](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/endpoints/zdr)). There is one new carve-out to disclose in Privacy. Since **Oct 27, 2026, OpenRouter screens all inference for CSAM, ZDR traffic included**, and "content flagged as potential CSAM may be retained for human review, reporting, and preservation" ([OpenRouter ZDR docs](https://openrouter.ai/docs/features/zdr)). This also means a player's prompt reaches the provider and OpenRouter's scanner before Aweborn's output check ever runs. A cheap **input-side hard-line check** therefore protects Aweborn's account, even though the decided policy rightly centers on outputs.

## Images: explicit stylized art needs a second provider and its own style gate

OpenRouter's image catalog is almost entirely closed first-party models (Microsoft MAI-Image, Google, OpenAI, BFL, xAI, ByteDance, Recraft, Krea) ([OpenRouter image guide](https://openrouter.ai/blog/tutorials/image-generation-models/)). None of them is a plausible source of explicit art, so **adult image generation means adding a provider beyond the current MAI path**. The realistic hosts run open-weight checkpoints with a safety checker you can switch off. On Replicate, disabling the checker through the API is explicitly meant for plugging in "a custom safety-checking model or a third-party service" ([Replicate safety docs](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/predictions/safety-checking.md)). Runware says NSFW filtering is configurable per model or per request ([Runware help](https://help.runware.ai/articles/5571280435-is-there-a-way-to-filter-out-nsfw-content)). ModelsLab sells NSFW API access from $21/month ([stablediffusionapi.com](https://stablediffusionapi.com/models/nsfw)).

fal is the tricky one. Its AUP bans non-consensual likeness and "any other harmful, violent, or sexually explicit content" ([fal AUP](https://fal.ai/legal/acceptable-use-policy)), yet its Trust & Safety page says it has adopted "a comprehensive NSFW content policy governing permissible and prohibited content" ([fal Trust & Safety](https://fal.ai/legal/trust-and-safety)). Those two statements conflict. Ask safety@fal.ai before relying on it **[Confirm in writing]**. Together's `disable_safety_checker` flag is reported only through a search snippet **[Unverified]**. Stability's AUP (effective Sep 30, 2026) **bans sexually explicit content even on self-hosted weights**, which rules out SD3.x for this use ([Stability AUP](https://stability.ai/use-policy)). xAI's Grok Imagine costs $0.02 per image ([xAI](https://x.ai/api/imagine)), but its API has no documented adult tier **[Unverified]**. It also sits at the center of 2026's deepfake crackdown, with an EU inquiry ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/06/elon-musks-grok-chatbot-deepfakes-nude-images-women-children/)), a California AG investigation ([The Record](https://therecord.media/california-grok-deepfakes-investigation)) and UK probes. That reputational baggage is a reason to skip it.

The model choice is a trade between license cleanliness and anime quality. **Chroma** (8.9B, FLUX-schnell base, Apache 2.0, billed "uncensored") ([HF Chroma](https://huggingface.co/lodestones/Chroma/blob/2daa38693a04d1b4936efe558246f84a9ac6cc73/README.md)), **FLUX.2 [klein] 4B** (Apache 2.0) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/black-forest-labs/FLUX.2-klein-4B)) and the original **Qwen-Image** (Apache 2.0) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image)) carry the least license risk. The SDXL anime family (Illustrious, NoobAI, Pony) is the strongest stylized ecosystem, and NoobAI's e621 tag coverage suits Aweborn's fantasy creatures ([aiofm](https://aiofm.info/en/guides/pony-vs-illustrious)). But its Fair-AI-Public-License variants say the model "cannot be used for money-making services" and are reported inconsistently ([aiofm](https://aiofm.info/en/compare/illustrious-vs-noobai-xl)). FLUX.2 [dev] and [klein] 9B are non-commercial. A free nonprofit service is not automatically "non-commercial" under these licenses **[Lawyer]**. Whether Stability's 2025 AUP reaches SDXL derivatives is contested **[Lawyer]**.

Cost isn't the constraint. Runware's FLUX schnell starts at **$0.0006 per image** ([Runware](https://runware.ai/models/bfl-flux-1-schnell)). Self-hosted SDXL on a rented RTX 4090 ($0.34–0.69/hr) works out to about **$0.00025–0.0005 per image at full use** ([runaihome](https://runaihome.com/blog/flux-vs-sdxl-vs-sd15-cost-per-image-2026/)), and roughly $0.0025 at 20% use (inferred). Retention is the constraint. **No data-retention or training terms were found for fal, Replicate, Runware, Together, ModelsLab or Novita**, so none of them can yet be called ZDR. Aweborn's single VPS has no GPU, so "self-hosting" here means a serverless GPU rental (RunPod, Modal) where Aweborn controls the image and the logs. That route fully meets the ZDR principle and removes ban risk. It also moves all moderation duty onto Aweborn, which it already accepts.

"Stylized only" has no off-the-shelf enforcer. Treat it as four layers. First, use booru-trained, non-photoreal checkpoints and avoid "realistic" merges. Second, rewrite prompts on the server: always prepend style tokens and always negative-prompt "photo, photorealistic", with no user override. Third, score every output with a photo-vs-illustration classifier and a hard threshold. city96's CityAesthetics is deliberately "completely negative towards real life photos" and can serve as a signal ([HF](https://huggingface.co/city96/CityAesthetics/blame/main/README.md)), Sightengine sells a `type` (photo vs. illustration) model ([Sightengine](https://sightengine.com/docs/image-moderation-principles)), and a small head on CLIP ViT-L/14 embeddings is cheap to train ([city96 AnimeClassifiers](https://huggingface.co/city96/AnimeClassifiers)). Fourth, run the age, likeness and CSAM checks below. No benchmark exists for photoreal-vs-stylized accuracy on AI art, so Aweborn has to build a small eval set of its own.

## 3D and audio: Meshy and Suno say no, ElevenLabs voice probably says yes

**Meshy, Aweborn's current 3D provider, bans "Pornographic material"** and runs an NSFW filter ([Meshy AUP](https://www.meshy.ai/acceptable-use-policy)). Its help-center filter article now returns 404, so it's unknown whether the filter scans image inputs. Hyper3D Rodin bans prompts that "include porn" ([Hyper3D Terms](https://hyper3d.ai/legal/terms)). Tripo's terms returned 403. Two Meshy terms matter today, whatever happens with 18+: **non-Enterprise API output is deleted after 3 days**, and Meshy may train on non-Enterprise content **unless you opt out by emailing support@meshy.ai** ([Meshy Terms §2.9](https://www.meshy.ai/terms-of-use)). Aweborn should send that opt-out now.

Explicit stylized 3D therefore means self-hosting an open-weight image-to-3D model. **TRELLIS (Microsoft) and TripoSR are MIT-licensed** **[Unverified, not re-fetched]** ([TRELLIS](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS); [TripoSR](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/TripoSR)). Hunyuan3D 2.1's license "DOES NOT APPLY IN THE EUROPEAN UNION, UNITED KINGDOM AND SOUTH KOREA" and has a vague "social ethics and moral standards" clause ([Hunyuan3D LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/tencent/Hunyuan3D-2.1/blob/main/LICENSE)). Stable Fast 3D and SPAR3D fall under Stability's sexual-content ban. Rigging is another gap: Meshy's auto-rig is covered by the same AUP, and Mixamo's terms on adult characters weren't checked. The pragmatic launch choice is to let 18+ worlds use Meshy for non-explicit 3D, keep explicit content to 2D pictures at first, and add a self-hosted TRELLIS path once the image style gate works. That path would render the 3D output to images so it goes through the same checks.

Voice is friendlier. ElevenLabs' Prohibited Use Policy (09 Oct 2026) bans "**Visual** depictions of explicit sexual acts", erotica involving incest, bestiality or necrophilia, non-consensual intimate content, and anything sexualizing minors. It also bans cloning any living person's voice without "explicit and informed consent" ([ElevenLabs Use Policy](https://elevenlabs.io/use-policy)). Read literally, **consensual adult erotic speech between adult characters is allowed**. The "visual" wording is unusual and could be tightened, so get ElevenLabs Trust & Safety to confirm Aweborn's use **[Confirm in writing]**, and copy its carve-outs exactly into Aweborn's hard lines. Cartesia ([AUP](https://cartesia.ai/legal/acceptable-use)) and Hume ([AUP](https://hume.ai/acceptable-use-policy)) ban only minor-related sexual content and non-consensual cloning, which makes them plausible backups. Cartesia also keeps an "objectionable, sole discretion" clause. Fish Audio bans sexually explicit submissions and trains on user content ([Fish Audio Terms](https://fish.audio/terms)), so rule it out. Self-hosted Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) and Chatterbox (MIT, watermarked) are the license-clean fallbacks. F5-TTS weights and XTTS-v2 are non-commercial **[Unverified, not re-fetched]** ([Kokoro](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M); [Chatterbox](https://github.com/resemble-ai/chatterbox)). No per-character prices or retention terms were retrieved for ElevenLabs, Cartesia or Hume.

Music matters less, because 18+ worlds need ambience more than explicit lyrics. **Suno bans sexually explicit audio** ([Suno Safety](https://suno.com/safety)), and Stability bans it for Stable Audio even when self-hosted. ElevenLabs Music sits under the same use policy, plus a plan-based commercial-rights table ([ElevenLabs Music Terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)). Keep ElevenLabs for music and sound effects, on a paid tier with commercial rights, and leave explicit lyrics off the menu. The decided "veiled planet" rule, which swaps in the world's seeded music, already assumes music is shared across tiers.

## Hash matching catches old material, so outputs need a custom age-and-likeness gate

The core technical finding is that **hash matching only finds material that has already been catalogued**. Model outputs are new by construction, so hashing does almost nothing for them ([Thorn](https://www.thorn.org/blog/how-thorns-csam-classifier-uses-artificial-intelligence-to-build-a-safer-internet/)). Hashing still belongs on the **upload path**, where someone may re-upload known real CSAM, and it costs close to nothing. **PhotoDNA Cloud is free for vetted UGC hosts** (images only, case-by-case approval, no SLA, unknown monthly cap) ([PhotoDNA FAQ](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/photodna/faq)). NCMEC offers a Hash Sharing API once you register, which ROOST's open-source Hasher-Matcher-Actioner can consume ([ROOST Coop NCMEC](https://roostorg.github.io/coop/1.0/integrations/ncmec.html)). Cloudflare's free CSAM Scanning Tool needs no NCMEC credentials since April 2025, but it only scans content served through the Cloudflare cache and reports matches by daily email after serving ([Cloudflare docs](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/reference/csam-scanning/)). It's a useful backstop, but **it cannot be the pre-display gate** that Aweborn's policy promises.

Classifiers that detect new CSAM exist, but they are gated or expensive. Thorn's Safer Predict (images, video, and text for grooming and sextortion) has a reported median spend of about **$50,000/yr**, based on only three purchases ([Vendr](https://vendr.com/marketplace/thorn)). Hive's Thorn-powered CSAM API is "Contact Sales" ([Hive](https://thehive.ai/apis/csam-detection)), and Hive's pricing page now redirects to deploy.com. Google's Content Safety API is free for approved partners and returns a priority score, not a verdict ([Google](https://protectingchildren.google/tools-for-partners/)). **None of them document performance on drawn or anime content**, so none of them can be assumed to enforce "apparent age in any art style." General guard models fall short too. ShieldGemma 2 has no child-specific category ([model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/shieldgemma/model_card_2)). Llama Guard 4 (12B, multimodal, on OpenRouter) includes S4 Child Sexual Exploitation ([Meta](https://www.llama.com/docs/model-cards-and-prompt-formats/llama-guard-4/); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/meta-llama/llama-guard-4-12b/api)), but how strict it is on stylized adult characters is unknown.

The age line therefore has to be built. On every sexual or nude output, run Llama Guard 4 as a first pass. Then run a vision-LLM judge with a published apparent-age rubric (body proportions, facial maturity, school or child context, stated age), hosted on a non-Anthropic model unless Anthropic confirms classification use. Anime taggers (WD14-family) and NudeNet are common add-ons, but their age-tag accuracy is unsourced **[Unverified]**. Any positive is refused. Borderline items wait, blurred, for a human. Before trusting this pipeline, Aweborn needs its own red-team eval set of adult and childlike characters across styles. Because the rubric and prompts will be public, this is also where the transparency promise will be tested hardest.

Real-person likeness is cheaper to catch. **AWS Rekognition RecognizeCelebrities and DetectModerationLabels each cost about $0.001 per image** at the first tier, with 5,000 free per month for 12 months. Each API is billed separately ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/whats-new/2021/11/amazon-rekognition-pricing-apis/)), and confirm current rates. Celebrity recognition only catches famous faces, so pair it with text-side checks that catch real names in prompts and captions. For private people, no proactive check works without reference images. The controls there are **StopNCII**, which any upload platform can join by emailing joinstopncii@swgfl.org.uk ([SWGfL](https://swgfl.org.uk/magazine/new-industry-partners-join-stopncii-org-to-prevent-the-sharing-of-non-consensual-intimate-images-online/)), and a fast takedown channel. The **TAKE IT DOWN Act's platform duties have been in force since May 19, 2026**: removal within 48 hours, reasonable efforts against identical copies, and FTC civil penalties of about $53,088 per violation ([Mondaq/Wiley](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/advertising-marketing-branding/1790166/may-19-deadline-for-take-it-down-act-compliance-is-your-company-prepared)). The statute's "covered platform" definition wasn't checked **[Lawyer]**. The stylized-only rule already removes most deepfake risk, since photoreal sexual output is refused outright.

For text and chat, use two tiers. A cheap guard (Llama Guard 4, or OpenAI's free omni-moderation, which has a `sexual/minors` category) screens everything. A rubric judge then covers what the guards lack: doxxing patterns, mass-casualty uplift, incitement, and Aweborn's exact carve-outs. OpenAI's moderation endpoint was once limited to OpenAI traffic, and whether third-party content is now allowed is disputed ([OpenAI community](https://community.openai.com/t/can-i-use-open-ai-moderation-api-agaist-non-open-ai-content-part-2/673566)) **[Confirm in writing]**.

The legal duty to report is fixed. Under 18 U.S.C. § 2258A, a service that learns of apparent CSAM must report it to NCMEC's CyberTipline. Register at esp.ncmec.org and obtain Reporting API credentials (secondary source: [dev.to](https://dev.to/sentinelsafety/ncmec-mandatory-reporting-for-online-platforms-what-developers-need-to-know-4k74)). The **REPORT Act (Pub. L. 118-59) extended preservation to one year** and requires storage consistent with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework ([Public Law 118-59](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/plaw/118/public/59)). In practice that means a separate encrypted bucket, access logging, and no route from normal app paths. **ROOST Coop** is free and open source, and provides the review queue, blurred review tooling and built-in NCMEC reporting a one-person team shouldn't build itself ([ROOST roadmap](https://roostorg.github.io/community/roadmap)). Two questions belong to a lawyer: whether purely stylized outputs that Aweborn blocks are "apparent CSAM" that must be reported, and how 18 U.S.C. § 1466A (obscene visual representations, which covers drawings) applies to a blocked-but-generated image **[Lawyer]**. A reasonable interim policy is to block every apparent-minor sexual output, and to report realistic ones and any user uploads. Excluding Thorn or Hive, the whole per-image stack (Rekognition moderation and celebrity checks, a guard call, a judge call, and free hashing) comes to **probably under $0.01 per image**. That figure is an estimate and hasn't been priced against current OpenRouter rates.

## Age checks: the law reaches further than the UK and the one-third states

Decision 3 limits real checks to the UK and to US states whose laws trigger at a third adult content. The research supports the UK half and **adds several jurisdictions**. Under the UK Online Safety Act, services allowing user-generated pornography had to have "highly effective age assurance" by July 2025. **Self-declaration and date-of-birth entry fail the standard.** Facial estimation, ID, credit-card and open-banking checks pass ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/age-checks-to-protect-children-online); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/01/16/a-new-age-ofcom-publishes-final-version-of-age-assurance-guidance-102ju6k)). Ofcom has fined small foreign porn operators up to £1.35m ([UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/news/government-and-policy/ofcom-issues-largest-ever-online-saftey-act-fine-20260223)). Most of those fines go unpaid, but Ofcom can also order ISP blocking and payment withdrawal ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/17/ofcom-discovers-issuing-online-safety-act-fines-is-easier-than-collecting-them/5297110)). The UK has no one-third safe harbor, so any explicit 18+ world open to UK users needs a check. Whether animated or AI pornography and explicit chatbot text are in scope wasn't confirmed from Ofcom's own text **[Lawyer]**.

The new additions matter most because of Aweborn's minds. **Australia's Age-Restricted Material Codes (in full effect since March 2026) explicitly cover AI companion chatbots that can generate sexually explicit material.** Such services must confirm users are 18+ or prevent that content, and an "I am 18" button no longer counts ([eSafety fact sheet](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-04/Age-restricted-Material-Codes-fact-sheet-April2026.pdf)). Chub AI chose to geoblock Australia instead ([Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202603/companion-chatbots-not-doing-enough-to-protect-kids-esafety-report)). France's SREN law and Arcom standard require at least two methods, one of them double-anonymous. Italy's AGCOM rule applies to 48 listed platforms, and its scope may grow. **Brazil's ECA Digital bans self-declaration, and administrative sanctions start this month (Nov 2026)** ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/age-verification-compliance-eu-uk/); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/04/enforcement-of-brazils-eca-digital-introduces-new-obligations-for-companies)). Germany's JMStV "closed user group" rule is background knowledge only **[Unverified]**. Canada's S-209 isn't law yet ([Didit](https://didit.me/blog/age-verification-canada/)). The EU age app is voluntary.

In the US, about **27 states** have adult-site laws, and *Free Speech Coalition v. Paxton* (June 2025) upheld the one-third model ([ageonce](https://www.ageonce.com/blog/us-state-age-verification-laws-2026); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Speech_Coalition_v._Paxton)). Three findings complicate a one-third bet. Utah's definition expressly covers "**animated**" depictions ([Utah 78B-3-1001](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/ut/78B-3-1001)), so stylized-only gives no exemption. Indiana counts "images and videos published on the website" ([Indiana 24-4-23-1](https://law.counselstack.com/statute/in/24-4-23-1)), so the share depends on what gets counted. And Tennessee (which has a criminal provision), Indiana, South Dakota and Missouri reportedly reach non-commercial sites ([ageonce](https://www.ageonce.com/blog/us-state-age-verification-laws-2026)). Whether one world counts as a "website", whether text, 3D and mind chats count as "material", and whether an 18+ path is its own site are all **[Lawyer]** questions. The full 27-state table wasn't verified against the FSC tracker **[Unverified]**. California SB 243 and New York's companion law apply to adult-only services too. They require AI disclosure and crisis protocols but no age checks. New York penalties reach $15,000/day, and California adds a private right of action ([MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai)). ## Conclusion

The 18+ plan holds up, but the research moves its weight. The hard part isn't finding adult-capable models. Open-weight text and image models are plentiful and cheap. The hard part is the **apparent-age judgment on stylized art**, which no vendor sells and which Aweborn will have to build, test and publish itself. That work is now the critical path, and it also makes Aweborn's transparency promise unusually valuable, because a public rubric and eval for "minors in any style" barely exists anywhere in the field. The second shift is that Aweborn's own minds make it a sexual-AI-companion service under Australian law, and probably a UK user-generated pornography service. That pulls more jurisdictions into the real-check list than Decision 3 assumed, and makes geo-triggered checks a launch requirement rather than a later fix.

One structural choice simplifies a lot: **running explicit image (and later 3D) generation on GPUs Aweborn controls**. It turns unknown host retention terms, vague AUPs and ban risk into one problem Aweborn already accepts, which is moderating its own outputs. It costs a fraction of a cent per image. Text can stay on OpenRouter's ZDR routing with a machine-generated menu, voice on ElevenLabs once confirmed, and the open-source ROOST tools can carry the reporting and review load a small team couldn't build alone.
