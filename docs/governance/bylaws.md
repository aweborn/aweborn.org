# Bylaws of Aweborn

A District of Columbia nonprofit corporation. Adopted by unanimous written consent of the Board of Directors on November 19, 2026.

> Published text of the Bylaws as adopted. Article 3.3 (independent majority) took effect on the Transition Date set in the adopting consent, when the new directors accepted office in November 2026. The signed consent is kept in Aweborn's records.

## Article 1. Name, office and purpose

1.1 **Name.** The corporation's name is Aweborn (the "Corporation").

1.2 **Office.** The Corporation's principal office is in the District of Columbia at the address the Board sets from time to time.

1.3 **Purpose.** The Corporation is organized and operated exclusively for the charitable, educational and scientific purposes stated in its Articles of Incorporation, within the meaning of Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code. If these Bylaws conflict with the Articles of Incorporation or the D.C. Nonprofit Corporation Act, the Articles and the Act control.

## Article 2. Members

The Corporation has no members. Any action that would otherwise require approval of members requires only approval of the Board of Directors.

## Article 3. Board of Directors

3.1 **Powers.** All corporate powers are exercised by or under the authority of the Board, and the Corporation's activities and affairs are managed under its direction.

3.2 **Number.** The Board has no fewer than three and no more than nine directors. Within that range, the Board sets the exact number by resolution. A decrease does not shorten a sitting director's term.

3.3 **Independence.** At all times after the effective date of these Bylaws, a majority of directors must be independent. A director is **independent** if the director (a) is not paid by the Corporation as an employee or contractor, other than reimbursement of reasonable expenses; (b) is not a family member of anyone the Corporation pays; and (c) has no material financial interest in any transaction with the Corporation. "Family member" means a spouse or domestic partner, ancestor, sibling (and their spouse), child, grandchild or great-grandchild (and their spouses). A temporary shortfall from a resignation does not invalidate Board action, but the Board must fill the seat with an independent director as soon as practical.

3.4 **Election and term.** Directors are elected by a majority vote of the directors then in office. Each director serves the term the Board sets when electing the director, which may be up to five years, the longest D.C. law allows, and until a successor takes office. Directors may be re-elected.

3.5 **Resignation.** A director may resign by written notice to the President or Secretary. The resignation takes effect when delivered unless the notice states a later date or event.

3.6 **Removal.** A director may be removed, with or without cause, by a vote of two-thirds of the other directors then in office.

3.7 **Vacancies.** The Board fills any vacancy, including one created by an increase in the number of directors, by a majority vote of the directors then in office, even if fewer than a quorum.

3.8 **No pay for board service.** Directors are not paid for serving as directors. They may be reimbursed for reasonable, documented expenses incurred for the Corporation. A director who is also an officer or employee may be paid for that work only as approved under Article 6.

## Article 4. Meetings and action of the Board

4.1 **Regular meetings.** The Board meets at least once a year, at an annual meeting held in December or January at a time and place it sets. At the annual meeting the Board elects directors and officers whose terms have ended, approves the coming year's budget and reviews the prior year's finances. The Board may hold other regular meetings as it decides.

4.2 **Special meetings.** The President or any two directors may call a special meeting with at least two days' notice to each director, stating the time, place and purpose. A director waives notice by attending without objecting or by signing a written waiver.

4.3 **Remote participation.** Directors may take part in any meeting by video or telephone, so long as all participants can hear each other at the same time. Participating this way counts as being present.

4.4 **Quorum and voting.** A majority of the directors then in office is a quorum. Unless these Bylaws say otherwise, the act of a majority of directors present at a meeting with a quorum is the act of the Board.

4.5 **Action without a meeting.** Any action the Board may take at a meeting may be taken without a meeting if each director signs a consent, on paper or electronically, describing the action, and the consents are delivered to the Corporation. Such action has the same effect as a vote at a meeting.

4.6 **Committees.** The Board may create committees of two or more directors and delegate authority to them as the D.C. Nonprofit Corporation Act allows. Any committee that sets compensation must consist only of independent directors.

## Article 5. Officers

5.1 **Offices.** The officers are a President, a Secretary and a Treasurer, and any others the Board creates. The Board elects officers for the terms it sets, up to five years, and they serve until successors are elected. One person may hold more than one office, except that the President may not also be the Treasurer.

5.2 **President.** The President is the chief executive officer, manages day-to-day operations under the Board's direction, and may use the title Executive Director.

5.3 **Secretary.** The Secretary may certify Board actions to others, such as a bank, when asked. The President keeps the Corporation's records, including minutes, written consents and signed forms.

5.4 **Treasurer.** The Treasurer reviews the Corporation's finances with the Board at each annual meeting. The President and the Corporation's accountant handle bookkeeping and tax filings.

5.5 **Removal.** The Board may remove any officer at any time by a majority vote of the directors in office.

## Article 6. Compensation and conflicts of interest

6.1 **Conflict-of-interest policy.** The Board shall adopt and keep in force a conflict-of-interest policy. Every director and officer signs an annual disclosure under it.

6.2 **Setting compensation.** Pay for any officer, employee or contractor who is a director, or a family member of a director, is set only by the independent directors, with the person concerned and any family member absent from the vote. Before deciding, the independent directors review pay data for similar roles at comparable organizations and record in the minutes or written consent, at the time of the decision, the terms approved, the data relied on and how each director voted.

6.3 **Reasonable compensation only.** No part of the Corporation's net earnings shall benefit any director, officer or other private person, except reasonable pay for services actually rendered and reimbursement of expenses.

## Article 7. Finances and records

7.1 **Fiscal year.** The fiscal year is the calendar year.

7.2 **Budget.** The Board approves an annual budget before or at the start of each fiscal year.

7.3 **Contracts and payments.** The Board may authorize any officer to sign contracts and make payments within the approved budget. Any single commitment over $5,000, and any payment to a director, officer or family member of one, other than approved pay, needs prior approval by the independent directors.

7.4 **Records.** The Corporation keeps permanently its Articles, Bylaws, minutes, written consents and annual returns, and keeps accurate financial records. It makes its exemption application and recent annual returns available for public inspection as federal law requires.

## Article 8. Indemnification

The Corporation shall indemnify and advance expenses to its directors and officers to the fullest extent the D.C. Nonprofit Corporation Act permits, and may buy insurance for that purpose.

## Article 9. Amendments

These Bylaws may be amended or repealed by a vote of two-thirds of the directors in office, at a meeting with at least seven days' notice of the proposed change or by unanimous written consent. Article 3.3 and Article 6 may be amended only with the approval of a majority of the independent directors.
