# Aweborn Conflict of Interest Policy

Adopted by the Board of Directors on November 19, 2026. Modeled on the IRS sample policy for 501(c)(3) organizations.

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects Aweborn when it considers a transaction or arrangement that might benefit the private interest of a director, officer or key employee, including setting anyone's pay. It supplements, and does not replace, the D.C. Nonprofit Corporation Act.

## 2. Definitions

**Interested person.** Any director, officer, or member of a committee with Board-delegated powers who has a direct or indirect financial interest.

**Financial interest.** A person has a financial interest if the person, or a family member, directly or through a business:

1. owns or invests in any entity with which Aweborn has a transaction or arrangement;
2. has a compensation arrangement with Aweborn or with any entity or person with which Aweborn has a transaction or arrangement; or
3. has a potential ownership, investment or compensation interest in any entity or person with which Aweborn is negotiating.

Compensation includes direct and indirect pay and gifts or favors that are not insubstantial. A financial interest is not necessarily a conflict; under Section 3 the Board decides whether one exists. "Family member" has the meaning in the [Bylaws](./bylaws.md), Article 3.3.

## 3. Procedures

1. **Duty to disclose.** An interested person must disclose the financial interest and all material facts to the Board before it considers the transaction or arrangement.
2. **Deciding whether a conflict exists.** After disclosure and any discussion with the interested person, the interested person leaves the meeting (or does not sign the consent) while the remaining directors decide whether a conflict exists.
3. **Handling a conflict.** The interested person may present information but does not take part in the deliberation or vote. If appropriate, the Board appoints a disinterested person to look into alternatives. After reasonable investigation, the disinterested directors decide by majority vote whether the transaction is in Aweborn's best interest, fair and reasonable, and whether a more advantageous arrangement could reasonably be obtained.
4. **Violations.** If the Board has reason to believe someone failed to disclose, it informs the person, hears their explanation, and takes appropriate corrective action.

## 4. Records

The minutes or written consent record: who disclosed what interest, whether the Board found a conflict, who was present for discussion and the vote, the content of the discussion including alternatives considered, and the vote.

## 5. Compensation

A director who receives pay from Aweborn, directly or indirectly, or whose family member does, may not vote on matters about that pay. Such a director may give information about it to the Board or committee. Pay is approved under Article 6.2 of the [Bylaws](./bylaws.md).

## 6. Annual statements

Each director and officer signs a statement each year affirming that they received, read and understood this policy, agree to comply, and understand that Aweborn is charitable and must engage mainly in activities that further its tax-exempt purposes. The statement lists any current financial interests (see the Director Acceptance and Disclosure form).

## 7. Periodic reviews

At least once a year the Board reviews whether pay arrangements are reasonable and based on competent survey information, and whether any partnerships, joint ventures or arrangements with management providers serve Aweborn's charitable purposes and do not result in private benefit.
