Where your gift goes

Last updated: October 27, 2026

Aweborn is a 501(c)(3) non-profit. Every dollar that comes in becomes mana in the universe’s pool, so the pool is a live picture of all the money Aweborn has. Every gift, from anyone, is shared out the same way, and half of it is the creative energy that lets every player bring their ideas to life.

The split

50% 20% 30%
  • 50% Creative share, for everyone The compute behind every creation in the game: meshes, terrain, sounds, whole worlds.
  • 20% Research Our mission: learning how people build trust and work well together. Read the plan.
  • 30% People & operations Running the servers, keeping players safe, support, and finding more supporters.

This is our planned split. Our board reviews it every year, and we’ll update this page whenever it changes.

How mana works

  • One mana is one cent. Every dollar in (gifts, card sales, grants) adds 100 mana to the pool. Every dollar out draws from it, and every draw is labeled: creations, servers, people, research.
  • Players create with the creative share. When a player manifests something, the universe draws exactly what the compute costs us, with no markup, and only from the creative half. Creations can never eat into salaries or servers.
  • If money ever runs short. Servers and people have to be paid to keep the universe running. If the rest of the pool can’t cover a bill, we take the difference from the creative share, so everyone creates a little more slowly for a while. It’s never hidden: each time, a labeled star shows how much and why, and new gifts refill the creative share as usual.
  • Mana is shared. Nobody owns mana, not even donors. The bigger the creative share, the faster everyone can create.
  • Everyone gets a daily allowance. So the pool lasts and everyone gets a turn, each player can draw only so much per day, and when many people create at once the creative share is split fairly between them. Making things from thought is for players who have signed in, free of charge and with no donation needed; anyone can explore and build with blocks and the shared library without signing in. The allowance also follows your local sun: up to 10% more around midday and up to 10% less around midnight where you are. These limits are tuned as we watch how people play.
  • An example: a $10 gift is about $9.41 after card fees. That adds about 941 mana to the pool, and about 470 of it is creative mana for the whole universe.
  • The universe grows. Every dollar in also pushes the Living Frontier outward. A golden wave sweeps from the center of the cosmos, and dreamed-up worlds become solid.

The pool’s books are live: see the current pool, every entry (amounts and dates, plus a donor’s chosen name only if they opted in) and what’s leaving the pool today. Until players create with it, the creative half pays for the servers and compute that already run the universe.

The living sky

You can watch the pool in the game. Nothing here is decoration: the sky is drawn from the same books.

  • How rich the sky looks follows how full the creative share is: bright and crowded with stars when it’s full, dimmer as it runs low, and nearly dark (with an aurora by the Portal) when it’s almost empty.
  • Every mana that leaves the pool leaves as a shooting star. A creation streaks up from the world it was made in, the moment it’s made. Bills (servers, people, fees) drift out of the Portal, spread evenly over the period they pay for, so a monthly bill becomes a gentle, steady drizzle instead of one burst. Small amounts are merged into one star so a busy day reads as a shower.
  • The colour is the IRS category the money falls under on our Form 990: gold for program services, blue for management and general, silver for fundraising. Tap a star to see what it paid for and its Form 990 line. The mana panel lists today’s stars by purpose.
  • Refunds are shown, too. If we refund a gift (or a bank reverses one), it leaves the pool as a labeled refund draw, never by quietly editing the books. The original gift stays in the ledger.
  • We watch the runway. We get a private early warning when the money outside the creative share would last less than 90 days at our recent spending, so we can act before a bill can’t be paid. The warning itself isn’t shown in the game; if a bill ever does dip into the creative share, that shows as a shortfall star, as described above.

The categories are our reading of the Form 990 instructions; our accountant will confirm them. If a category changes, it changes here and in the game together.

What a gift doesn’t buy

No mana of your own, no abilities, no levels, no advantage over anyone. Abilities are earned by playing. The one thing we offer donors is a thank-you you can opt into when you give (we’re switching this option on soon): your name (up to 20 characters) on the golden wave (“A wave of light from ★ you”) and next to your gift in the public ledger. Without it, the wave says “a generous soul”.

Why half

  • Most of every gift goes to the mission. Mana and research together are 70%. The BBB Wise Giving Alliance asks charities to spend at least 65% on their programs and no more than 35% of contributions on fundraising (BBB Standards for Charity Accountability).
  • But not by starving the organization. When non-profits squeeze overhead to look lean, they underinvest in the people and systems they need to do good work (Gregory & Howard, “The Nonprofit Starvation Cycle”, Stanford Social Innovation Review, 2009).
  • One honest number. Donors care where their money goes, and giving rises when people know their gift isn’t paying for overhead (Gneezy, Keenan & Gneezy, “Avoiding overhead aversion in charity”, Science, 2014). So we keep the split simple and public, and if a founding grant ever covers our operations, we’ll raise the mana share of everyone else’s gifts.
  • Impact, not just ratios. Overhead ratios are easy to judge and impact is hard, so people tend to over-weight the ratio (Caviola et al., “The evaluability bias in charitable giving”, Judgment and Decision Making, 2014). That’s why you can see your gift in the game.
